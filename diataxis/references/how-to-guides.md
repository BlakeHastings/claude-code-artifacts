# Writing how-to guides (goal-oriented)

A how-to guide gives "directions that guide the reader through a problem or towards a result."
It serves *application* through *action*: a competent user who already has the basics and now
has a specific real-world goal. The recipe is the model: it answers a question, assumes baseline
competence, and stays laser-focused on getting the result.

## How it differs from a tutorial

Never conflate them. A tutorial teaches a beginner and the author owns the goal; a how-to helps
an already-competent user reach *their* goal. "How-to guides must be written from the
perspective of the user, not of the machinery." Meaning comes from the user's purpose, not from
what the system can do.

## It must

- **Address a real-world problem or task**, and stay adaptable to variations of it.
- **Describe a logical sequence** of actions in sensible order.
- **Seek flow.** Ground the sequence in how the user actually works and thinks.
- **Use conditional imperatives** for the branches real problems have: "If you want X, do Y";
  "If Z happened instead, do W."
- **Begin and end in meaningful places.** Start where the user realistically starts; stop when
  the goal is met. Let them slot it into their own context.

## It must not

- **Teach or explain.** No fundamentals, no background, no theory.
- **Be exhaustive.** "Practical usability is more helpful than completeness." Omit the
  unnecessary; assume competence.
- **Flatten real complexity** into a single rigid linear path when the task genuinely branches.
- **Describe the machinery for its own sake.** Focus on the user's goal, not a tour of features.

## Voice, titles, and links

- **Title says exactly what it shows, starting with "How to":** "How to integrate application
  performance monitoring." Avoid vague titles and titles without "how."
- Imperative, direct: "Create the bucket. Grant the role. Verify access."
- Push detail out to reference: "Refer to the X reference guide for the full list of options."

## Checklist

- [ ] Title is "How to <specific real task>."
- [ ] Assumes the reader is competent; no teaching of basics.
- [ ] Solves a problem the user actually has, from their perspective.
- [ ] Handles real branches with conditional imperatives; not artificially linear.
- [ ] No theory or exhaustive description; links out for both.
- [ ] Starts and ends where it is useful to the reader.
