# Writing explanation (understanding-oriented)

Explanation (also called discussion) deepens understanding. It serves *acquisition* through
*cognition*: it is read at leisure, away from the keyboard, and answers "Can you tell me
about…?" It is the widest-angle mode, the place for the why and the bigger picture.

## It must

- **Provide context and background**: the reasons behind a design, the history, the technical
  constraints, how the parts relate to each other and to the wider world.
- **Make connections.** Tie the subject to related concepts and to topics outside the immediate
  product. Weave understanding rather than list facts.
- **Discuss alternatives and counter-examples.** Weigh the options that were considered and the
  trade-offs taken.
- **Admit opinion and perspective.** Understanding inherently involves a viewpoint; explanation
  is allowed to argue and to judge, which the other three modes are not.
- **Talk about the subject** from above, rather than walking the user through it.

## It must not

- **Instruct.** No step-by-step procedures; that is a tutorial or how-to.
- **Serve as reference.** No exhaustive machinery-level description.
- **Take the user's eye-level, in-the-task perspective.** Explanation steps back.
- **Sprawl.** "Keep explanation closely bounded" so it does not absorb the other three modes.
  This is the mode most likely to leak everywhere; fence it in.

## Voice and patterns

- Reflective and discursive: "X exists because…", "One approach is…, another is…", "Historically
  this was… which is why…".
- It can be read on its own, without the product open.
- Link to the how-to guides and reference it discusses, rather than restating them.

## Checklist

- [ ] Illuminates the why, the context, and the trade-offs.
- [ ] Makes connections to related ideas and the broader picture.
- [ ] Contains no procedure and no exhaustive description.
- [ ] Is honest about alternatives and opinionated where useful.
- [ ] Stays bounded to its topic; does not creep into the other modes.
- [ ] Makes sense read away from the product.
