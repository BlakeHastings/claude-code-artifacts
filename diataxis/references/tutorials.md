# Writing tutorials (learning-oriented)

A tutorial is **a lesson**: a practical activity in which a newcomer acquires skill by doing,
under your guidance. It serves *acquisition* through *action*. The user's question is not yet
"how do I do X" but "teach me to use this."

## The teaching contract

In a tutorial the teacher bears almost all the responsibility: "the teacher has responsibility
for what the pupil is to learn, what the pupil will do in order to learn it, and for the
pupil's success." The learner's only job is to follow attentively. You choose the path; they
must not have to make decisions or research anything.

## It must

- **Deliver a meaningful, complete, successful experience.** Exercises should be "meaningful,"
  "successful," "logical," and "usefully complete."
- **Show visible results early and often.** Each step produces something the learner can see,
  so confidence compounds.
- **Aspire to perfect reliability.** A tutorial "must inspire confidence"; if a step fails for
  the learner, the lesson breaks. Test it on clean environments repeatedly.
- **Stay concrete.** Move "from step to concrete step." Particular commands, particular output.
- **Be repeatable.** The learner can run it again and get the same result.

## It must not

- **Explain.** "Ruthlessly minimise explanation." Link to an explanation page instead of
  pausing the lesson.
- **Offer options or alternatives.** Ignore them; pick one path and walk it.
- **Abstract or generalize.** No "in general you might…". Stay in the concrete instance.
- **Demand decisions.** The learner should never have to choose or look something up.

## Voice and patterns

- Frame the destination up front: "In this tutorial, we will build and deploy…", not "you will
  learn about…".
- First person plural: "we will…", "now we add…".
- Set and confirm expectations: "You will notice that…", "The output should look like…".
- Direct attention: "Notice that…", "Remember that…".

## Checklist

- [ ] A beginner with no prior context can complete it start to finish.
- [ ] Every step works, verified on a clean setup; no step depends on unstated knowledge.
- [ ] Results are visible early and throughout.
- [ ] No alternatives, no theory, no "why" beyond a one-line pointer or link.
- [ ] The learner makes zero independent decisions.
- [ ] It ends at a clear, satisfying, complete result.
