# Consolidation: Outcomes & Understanding

The payoff of the sweep is the distilled read, not the raw log. Lift it up so the conclusion is legible without scrolling.

**Who concludes (read first).** You aggregate and organize the evidence, and the user draws the conclusion (SKILL.md principle 8). Consolidation is not where you deliver a verdict, it is where you hand the user a well-organized, evidence-linked basis to reach their own. Write the Outcomes as the current read of the evidence, not a ruling, and itemize every material conclusion in a `Claims to Confirm` checklist (below) that the user signs off on claim by claim. Do not record a conclusion the user has not seen and approved.

## Outcomes (top of the investigation note)
A section at the top of the investigation note, above the Raw Log. Structure:
- **Bottom line** — one `> [!important]` callout: the honest one-sentence verdict for the user's decision.
- **What is reassuring** — the legitimate positives. Include them for honesty and balance; this is due diligence, not a hit piece.
- **Concerns** — the meat: each a bolded point with a one-line explanation, **linked to its evidence note**. Order by how much they bear on the decision.
- **Questions to ask / next decisions** — concrete follow-ups (for a facility, what to ask the case manager; for a vendor, what to ask in diligence).

Keep it sourced, every concern traces to a reference note, and keep it current as the investigation evolves. Phrase the read as provisional ("the evidence points to X, pending your confirmation"), not as a settled verdict ("X is proven / resolved").

## Claims to Confirm (the user's sign-off checklist)
A section directly under `Outcomes`, above the Raw Log. It restates every material conclusion as a discrete, checkable claim the user verifies one by one, so nothing reaches "settled" without the user's own read. This is the mechanism for principle 8.

- One `- [ ]` checkbox per claim (the vault has the Tasks plugin, so the user ticks the box once satisfied).
- **Tag each claim by confidence type** so the user knows what kind of checking it needs:
  - **[record]** stated verbatim in a primary record, spot-check the cited proof note / source URL.
  - **[site]** visible on the live site or a captured screenshot, look at the page or the `Files/` image.
  - **[inference]** your reading of combined evidence, a judgment call for the user to weigh. This is where over-claiming hides, label it honestly, never launder an inference into a fact.
  - **[negative]** an absence / "searched and found none" result, confirm by re-running the search.
- **Link the exact artifact** for each claim (the proof note, the screenshot filename, the source URL), so the user can read the evidence without hunting for it.
- Group by tag, and call out any discrepancy that undercuts a claim (e.g. a record's expiration date conflicting with the site's) as its own item, do not bury it.
- Items not yet investigated at all are gaps, not claims, leave them in `Open Questions / Next Steps`, not here.

## Understanding (the project note)
When the investigation feeds a larger decision (e.g. choosing among several options), lift the cross-investigation understanding into the project note so options can be compared side by side. The individual investigation notes remain the detailed backing.

## Tone
Factual and fair. State concerns plainly with their evidence; do not editorialize beyond what the sources support; flag uncertainty as uncertainty. The goal is a decision the user can defend, not a verdict you have pre-decided.
