# Verification & Confidence Discipline

Overclaiming is the failure mode to guard against. Treat every nontrivial claim as a question of evidence.

## Label every nontrivial claim
- **Confirmed** — a primary or official record (registry, accreditor, the subject's own filing).
- **Likely** — strong circumstantial evidence, but no single dispositive document.
- **Plausible** — one weak signal (e.g. name + town); could be coincidence.
- **Unverified** — asserted somewhere, not yet checked.

State the basis for the label. The reference (source) note holds the proof; the claim points to it.

## Identity discipline (the common-name trap)
Names repeat, especially within tight-knit communities. Before equating two identities, require a **hard link**: a shared document, a cross-reference, a matching unique identifier (phone / address / license number), a mutual exclusive connection, or a clear photo match. Name + town is *plausible*, not confirmed.
- When several same-named people exist, enumerate them and rule each in or out explicitly.
- Capture the reasoning in a **link / identity note**: the evidence for, the evidence against, the current verdict, and what would confirm or refute it. Embed the proof.

## Sever and quarantine the unconfirmed
If an entity or account is only a plausible match, keep it in its OWN note, marked unconfirmed, and OUT of the investigation's aggregations (do not back-link it to the investigation). Reach it only through the link/identity note. Promote it (merge, or re-wire into the investigation) only once confirmed. This keeps unverified material from masquerading as fact.

## Listed somewhere ≠ active / true now
Being *named* in a record is not proof of the current real-world relationship it implies. A person can be the "contact" or responsible party on a facility's **active** state license (carried forward, un-updated, on each renewal) while actually working full-time elsewhere. A name on a roster, a license, or a directory is a *claim*, scoped to whenever that record was last touched.
- To confirm a person's **current** employment/role, go to an independent, more-current source: their current employer's own staff/provider directory, and their CMS NPI **practice address** (with its "last updated" date), not the record that merely names them.
- Worked example from this vault: a clinic's state-license contact was confirmed via the other org's provider directory + his NPI to be the full-time director at a *different* employer, so the license name was a stale leftover. The honest framing: "named on the license but appears to work elsewhere," not "no longer works there" (a documented-elsewhere fact does not prove zero tie to the first org). Keep the distinction between *documented* and *inferred* explicit in the note.

## Sub-agent findings are claims, not proof, especially specific IDs
A research sub-agent's report is *unverified* until you check it, however confident or precise it reads. The dangerous failure mode is a **fabricated specific**: a registry ID, accreditation date, or license number that looks authoritative but does not resolve. Observed in this work, a sub-agent reported a Joint Commission HCO ID and accreditation decision date that did not check out (an implausibly low ID the live lookup would not resolve), which nearly shipped into a reader-facing report.
- Before any specific identifier or date from a sub-agent enters the dossier or a reader-facing report, **confirm it against the primary record yourself** (the registry page, the accreditor's own report, the board lookup).
- **Capturing the primary source does double duty:** it backs the true claims with proof *and* exposes the fabricated ones. Pulling the actual record (e.g. the accreditor's PDF) both validated the real findings and caught the invented ID.
- Run an **adversarial validation pass** on multi-agent research before it goes out: re-verify the load-bearing, falsifiable claims (IDs, dollar figures, settlements, legal/regulatory assertions) against authoritative primary sources, and label anything you cannot independently confirm as Unverified rather than passing it through.

## When challenged, downgrade honestly
If the user doubts a claim, re-examine it from scratch and correct the notes, downgrade the confidence label, add the caveat, sever the entity, rather than defending the original call. A correction is cheap; a wrong "fact" baked into the dossier is not.
